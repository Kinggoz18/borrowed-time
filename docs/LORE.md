# Borrowed Time: lore (ongoing, deliberately unfinished)

*Working notes. Some of this is true, some of it is what the colonists believe, and we haven't decided which is which. Leave the gaps open; future seasons and other players fill them.*

## The colony

There were forty-one of us and a goat called Margery, on a ferry called *Patience* that was not built for open water.

We came from **Aster, the Metered City**. In Aster every hour is counted. You pay for a lamp by the hour, for a bench by the hour, and for the time a clerk spends looking at your papers. Children learn to read from their parents' hour-ledgers. Nobody in Aster is poor in money; they are poor in time. Most of us were behind. Some were very far behind.

We didn't flee the debt so much as the counting. Old Tobias says he hadn't sat down without paying for it in nine years. Nell, who steered the ferry, says she just wanted one afternoon that belonged to her.

We landed on an empty island with eight Hours between us, and nothing else.

## The island

The island is a sundial. You can see it from the hill: the old stone needle in the middle, the hour-ticks worn into the rock, the way the shadow walks across the land through the day. Whoever cut it is long gone.

Out here, light is something you can hold. A day's work leaves Hours behind, in the purses and in the walls. We don't understand it. Ada (who was a clockmaker's apprentice in Aster, and won't let anyone forget it) thinks the dial *keeps* time the way a cistern keeps rain. Others think it is simply generous. Nobody has asked the island.

**Why land goes grey:** when you borrow an hour, it has to come from somewhere. The lent land loses its colour, starting with the shore. The grass is still there, but its hours are working somewhere else. People who walk on grey land say it feels like the moment just before you remember something you forgot.

## The lender

She was already here when we landed, in a striped tent on the eastern shore with a hand-painted hourglass sign: **Hesper, the Clockkeeper**.

She lends daylight. Borrow from her and the evening stretches; you can finish the roof before dark. Then tomorrow comes up short, and she is there at dusk with her ledger, adding a little more.

What we know:
- She is polite and has never raised her voice.
- She takes what is owed "gently, as always". The first time she took a building, she thanked us for it.
- Her ledger is thick. Ada glimpsed a page: other islands' names, columns of hours, and some of the names crossed out with a single neat line.
- She once called Margery the goat by name. Nobody had told her.

When she collects, she leaves her **seal** on the island: a wax hourglass on the gnomon. For two seasons after, the dial won't overflow, however much we keep. "It's only a formality," she says. It's the one formality the island obeys.

What we don't know: whether she works for someone, whether she is from Aster, what she does with the hours, and what happens to an island when the last line is drawn.

## The raiders: the Late

They come at dusk in grey-sailed boats. They want hours, not blood. They take what we've stored and burn what's easiest to reach, which is always the grey land on the shore.

The children call them **the Late**. The name stuck because it is accurate. They are colonists like us, from islands that went fully grey, where the debt took every hour and there was no tomorrow left to borrow against. They only exist at dusk, between one day and the next, because that is all the time they have left.

Some of them know our names. One of them was singing a song from Aster, from the docks, the one about the lamplighter. Nell stopped fighting when she heard it, and we lost the Watchtower that night.

We never know which night they'll come. The watchtower can vouch for an empty horizon, so a quiet evening gets its call, *"Calm sea."*, but nobody can vouch for what's beyond it. On the other nights the sea only hints: the gulls go quiet, oars carry over the water, sometimes drums. Ada says you learn to read it the way you learn to read a face.

When we hold, they leave things behind: hours spilled on the beach, and sometimes a boat. We keep the boats. They moor at the docks and fish, and nobody asks whose they were. When we don't hold, they take hours and knock walls down, but never the frames. Rebuilding what they broke costs half of building it new. *They're here to take time, not timber.*

At the end of every season comes **the Long Dusk**: the shadow of everything owed, all at once. The more we owe, the bigger it rises. When we held it the first time, it didn't die. It sank back into the sea like it was patient.

## How the island grows (the tiers)

The island grows when it has **kept enough of our time** (see "Why time moves on" below). When it does, the dial seems to breathe out overnight: the shore pushes back, new ground surfaces from the shallows, and the palisade has to be re-cut to fit. Ada says the dial is giving us more room to keep time in. Tobias says it is just making more shore for Hesper to grey.

Every growth brings something new we can do, and every one of those things is, in the end, a way of dealing with borrowed time.

- **Colony (7×7).** The founders. Six came off the *Patience* on the first trip; Nell went back for the rest, and by the end of the first days all forty-one were ashore. The island holds no more than that yet. *Beat: "Forty-one people, one goat, and an island that keeps its own time."*
- **Village (11×11).** Someone painted a door blue, and a sail from another dial asked what we'd take for an hour. *Beat: "Someone painted a door blue, and a sail from another dial asked what we'd take for an hour."*
  - **Roads.** We laid them along the dial's own hour-lines, so the shadow walks down them like a lamplighter. Work moves faster along a road: people get to the fields while the morning is still worth something, instead of spending it walking. So does everything else: the Late use them too. *Every road runs both ways.* (More Hours from every building; raiders burn one more.)
  - **Trade Post.** Sails from other dials buy and sell hours, and the price changes with the tide. You stake Hours on a caravan at dusk and it comes back at morning worth more or less. You can stake borrowed Hours, and some nights that's clever. In a lean season the same sails sell grain, at their price. *Time is money. Here, literally.*
- **Town (15×15).** The first child born here asked what Aster was. Ada opened a school so somebody could answer. *Beat: "The first child born here asked what Aster was. Ada opened a school so somebody could answer."*
  - **Academy.** Ada's school, which mostly teaches clocks. Research costs Hours up front and pays every day after: cheaper interest (ledgers), better harvests (rotation), crossbows, faster looms. It is the one thing worth going into debt for, if you're sure. Studies finish overnight.
  - **Hospital.** The founders call it the Stitch. People caught on grey land when the Late come are carried there, and most of them come back. *A stitch in time saves nine,* or, on a good night, forty. The wounded come back in a sling after two days instead of being lost, and when the fever comes off the trade boats, the Stitch is where it stops.
- **City (21×21).** Lights on the horizon answered ours. From the new dome we watched the Long Dusk breathe. *Beat: "Lights on the horizon answered ours. From the new dome we watched the Long Dusk breathe."* The city spills past the wall now: farms, mills, and the docks.
  - **Exchange.** A counting house, built (Tobias spat when he saw it) on the plan of the ones in Aster. It refinances what we owe Hesper: cheaper interest and a longer line of credit. *Buy some time, at a discount.* Nobody asks who is on the other side of the ledger.
  - **Harbour.** A navy, of sorts, with the flagship ***Nick of Time***. When we borrow the dusk, the boats row out to meet the Late, and the borrowed light hits harder (×1.5 instead of ×1.3).
  - **Observatory.** A dome on the hill where Ada watches the Long Dusk gathering out at sea. She can tell skiffs from longboats and count the oars, roughly. She can't count intentions, so the Late can still surprise us, just less. The dome seems to make the Long Dusk smaller, too: it feeds less on what we owe. *Now we can watch time fly.*

  - **The Great Dial.** The city starts building its own dial, one ring, gear and lamp at a time, so it can keep its own time without Hesper. She won't lend a single hour toward it: *"I don't finance competition."* It is never finished. *It's only a matter of time.*

## Why time moves on (the ages)

The dial keeps time the way a cistern keeps rain. Every hour we live and work here without lending it away stays in the island: in the purses, the walls, the soil. The more of us there are, and the longer we've lived well, the more it holds.

When it holds more than the shore can carry, it **overflows**. One night the kept years spill into the island itself. We wake *later*. Not older: later. New ground has come up from the shallows, the tents are thatch, and the tools in the sheds are newer than the ones we put there. The dial spends our kept time on us.

That's why lending matters. Hours lent to Hesper aren't kept; they're working somewhere else. An island that owes past its limit has nothing of its own left to overflow. Ada says Aster never ages because it counts every hour and keeps none.

- **Wreck & Frontier (Colony).** The *Patience*'s hull is our first roof. Canvas, rope, driftwood. The fiddle at night is Tobias, badly.
- **Hearth & Harvest (Village).** One morning the tents were thatch. Nobody remembers building the mill. The bell rings at dusk now; we don't know who rings it.
- **Gears & Gilt (Town).** Brick, domes and the first clock tower. Ada wept, then started taking it apart.
- **Brass & Steam, the Meridian age (City).** Glass domes, gaslight, trams running down the hour-lines. It is beautiful, and it looks more like Aster every season. *Are we building what we ran from?*

Things that never change: Hesper's striped tent (she lives outside the overflow; her hours are always lent), the gnomon, and the Late's boats, which come from every age at once, each from the moment its island went grey and stopped keeping time.

Inside each age, buildings still improve every three levels, from the humble version of that age to its landmark. Big ones spread across the plots behind them, cottages lean together into streets, and the palisade climbs from stakes to stone with the age.

## Work, food and leaving

The dial pays for work, not for presence. Everyone has a home and a place to work: a field, the Clockworks, the Trade Post, a wall post. They leave at dawn when the gnomon's shadow touches the first hour-line, walk the roads, work, and come home at dusk. At night only the watch is out. Nobody wanders; there's no time to.

People are also the colony's last defence. A wall is only as strong as the people standing on it.

Fields feed people, and nothing else does. When there isn't enough, the hungry stop working first. By the third morning, families start leaving at dawn, rowing back toward the lights on the horizon. Feed them and they stop.

The island never empties. The first six off the *Patience* never leave the dial, whatever happens. If too many go, the newest streets go dark and grey: nothing burns, nothing is taken. The lamps relight when people come back.

## People with names

- **The founder (you).** One of the forty-one. You name them. They appear in the chronicle, never die, and become the Elder at City.
- **Nell** steered the *Patience*. **Tobias** hadn't sat down for free in nine years. **Ada** was a clockmaker's apprentice. Other notables come and go, each with one line and a small story: *"Wren mends nets and wants a boat."* → the Harbour opens → *"Wren sails the Nick of Time."*
- **Hesper keeps a second ledger, of trust.** Pay on time and she adds a seal; a seizure costs one. With enough seals she says things she shouldn't: an island's name, a date, once our own names in a hand that wasn't hers.
- **The Late have captains.** *Captain Ilse of the grey* Wren *(a different* Wren*; nobody likes that).* *Old Fennick, who sings the lamplighter song.* Beat a captain three times and they stop rowing. What happens next is open (question 2).
- **Margery** is still never there at dusk. Sometimes she is in the outskirts, standing on an old dial stone that wasn't there yesterday.

## The chronicle

Ada keeps a book. One line per thing that mattered: *"Season 4, Day 6: held the Long Dusk against Captain Ilse. Owed nothing at dawn."* Each age starts a new chapter, with our crest on the cover. Hesper has asked to read it. Twice.

## The wider world (hints, for later)

- **Other dials.** The lights on the horizon trade with us now, through the Trade Post, but we have never seen their faces. Some keep different ages from ours. (Future multiplayer: more colonies of our own, voyages, alliances that lend each other light, and raids. Some of the Late may be other players' grey islands.)
- **Bottles.** Messages wash up: a recipe for bread, a list of names, one that just says *don't borrow on the sixth day.*
- **Aster is still counting.** A ship with a clerk aboard has been seen, measuring the coast. The Exchange's ledgers are ruled in Aster's style.
- **Hesper's other tents.** A trader swears he saw the same striped tent on a different island, on the same night.
